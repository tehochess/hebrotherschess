How we coach

Most players plateau because they don't know their weaknesses.

They keep showing up to tournaments, repeating the same mistakes, not making progress. Our job is to find those gaps, give you targeted material to address them, and build the kind of practical skill that holds up in real games — not just in puzzles. We use engines extensively and know how to translate what they show into things a human can actually apply.

Getting started

The assessment

Before any coaching begins, we run a structured diagnostic to get a clear picture of where you are. This shapes everything that follows.

A
Pattern recognition Timed tactical puzzles across mixed themes — forks, pins, mating nets. Shows how sharp your pattern recognition is under pressure.
B
Position evaluation Static positions, no moves allowed. Who's better and why? Identify weak squares, pawn structure, best plans for both sides.
C
Prophylaxis What does your opponent want to do? How do you stop it? A lot of players skip this entirely.
D
Advanced puzzles Harder problems that require a move plus a full explanation. Wrong answers are as informative as right ones.
E
Self-evaluation Your honest read on your own game — comfort zones, strengths, areas you avoid. Useful data.

Sessions are held online on Lichess. It's free, works in the browser, and makes sharing positions and PGNs straightforward.

We recommend having at least a year of experience and a USCF rating around 1000 before starting. Players up to 2200 have gotten useful work out of the program.

Send us your recent tournament games along with the assessment if you have them — the more we see, the better the picture.
