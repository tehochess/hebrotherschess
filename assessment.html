Getting started

The assessment

Before any coaching begins, we run a structured diagnostic to get a clear picture of where you are. This shapes everything that follows — what we work on, what material we use, and how sessions are structured.

A
Pattern recognition Timed tactical puzzles across mixed themes — forks, pins, mating nets. Shows how sharp your pattern recognition is under real pressure, not just when you know it's a tactics problem.
B
Position evaluation Static positions, no moving pieces allowed. Who's better and why? Identify weak squares, pawn structure features, the worst piece on the board, and best plans for both sides.
C
Prophylaxis What does your opponent want to do? How do you stop it? A lot of players at every level skip this step entirely. It shows up clearly in games.
D
Advanced puzzles Harder problems that require a move plus a full explanation. Wrong answers are as informative as right ones — the reasoning reveals a lot about blind spots.
E
Self-evaluation Your honest read on your own game — comfort zones, what you avoid, how you feel in sharp vs. quiet positions. We compare this against what we see in the other sections.
Format

Conducted online via Lichess. Free, no download required, works in the browser. Makes sharing positions and PGNs simple.

Send us your recent tournament games along with the assessment if you have them — the more we see, the better picture we get.

Who it's for

We recommend at least a year of chess experience and a USCF rating around 1000 before starting. Players up to 2200 have gotten meaningful work out of the program.
